A family wall calendar that runs on the tablet you already own.

Porchlight turns an old iPad into a warm, glanceable home screen: both of your calendars in one place, Yo-Yo's walks with credit to whoever did them, and a photo diary of what you eat that lands right on the calendar. The MVP is a single iPad app with no server, and nothing about your household is hard-coded.

What changed in v2, from your feedback: Sunday Paper is the visual direction. No server for now: the iPad is the whole product, and phones and multi-device sync come later. The sample household is now two adults plus Yo-Yo. New: a food photo diary captured with the iPad camera and shown on the calendar.

Where the incumbents fall short

  • Paywall fatigue. The most-cited complaint is that the delightful parts (rewards, meals, photos, AI) sit behind Plus.
  • Generic personality. The UI is clean but clip-art friendly; nothing feels like your household.
  • No story of daily life. Pets, meals and small rituals are plain checkboxes at best, with no history to look back on.
  • Closed hardware. You buy their screen; an idle iPad cannot join.

2. Product principles

Glance, then tap

Every screen answers "what's happening now and next" from across the room. Logging anything is one or two taps.

The household is data

People, companions, journals, routines and caption tone are configured, never compiled in. Yo-Yo is a row in our own instance.

Life lands on the calendar

Walks and meals appear in the same timeline as events, so the calendar becomes a record of the week, not just a plan.

One source of truth

Events stay in Google / iCloud / Outlook. Porchlight reads and decorates them; it never forks the data.

3. Core concepts (domain model)

Everything on screen is driven by these entities. The two delight features share one idea: a log of small moments, attached to a day and a person.

Household

Timezone, week start, locale, units, theme. One per install today; a clean boundary for multi-device later.

Person

Name, color, avatar. No login on the wall: you tap your face to get credit.

CalendarSource

A calendar found through a CalendarProvider. The MVP provider is eventkit (every account on the iPad); ics links are a second one. Each source maps to a person or "Both", with visibility full or busy.

Event

Read live from the provider and decorated locally with an emoji / sticker and a countdown flag.

Companion → CareRoutine → CareLog

A companion has a species preset and look. A routine has a cadence (2× daily), time windows, a rotation and tracked fields (duration, pee, poo, vibe). A log records who, when, and those fields.

Journal → Entry

A log with slots (Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Snack) and a source (Home-cooked or Delivery; eating out later). An entry has an optional photo, an optional Apple Pencil note, the slot, who ate, and a rating. The food diary is a Journal; a "garden" or "outfits" journal would be another.

Chore → Completion

Recurring task with an owner, a rotation or "anyone", a star value and a due window. Care routines can spawn chores ("Feed Yo-Yo dinner").

List / Reward / Album

Grocery and to-do lists, shared star goals ("Date night fund"), and the photo source for Ambient mode.

4. Architecture: one native iPad app

Dropping the server changes the right technology. A web page cannot read the iPad's calendars, keeps Google sign-in tokens alive only with a server, and has weaker camera and always-on support. A native SwiftUI app gets all of that from the operating system.

Calendars

Camera capture with a smart meal slot, optional Apple Pencil notes, a handwritten paper-style month (write straight into each day), polaroid week, plates on Today / Week / Month, weekly stats and "on this day" memories.

Chores with stars and shared goals, grocery and to-do lists, Ambient photo mode with night schedule.

CloudKit sync, an iPhone app (log walks and meals from anywhere), Android or web, meal planning, AI import of flyers and emails.

6. Risks to plan around

  • Which calendars the iPad can see. Your wife's calendar appears if her account is added to the iPad or she shares her calendar with an account that is. Setup should explain this in one friendly screen.
  • Free Apple ID. Installs expire every 7 days, so the Mac mini rebuilds and reinstalls weekly over the network (needs Xcode selected as the active developer directory, which takes your password once). A free account also cannot use iCloud / CloudKit, so the later sync phase will need the paid account.
  • Old iPad hardware. The 2017 iPad Pro tops out at iPadOS 17 and its battery is likely tired. Plugged in 24/7 it can swell: use a smart plug to cycle charging and keep it ventilated.
  • Photo storage. Three meals a day adds up. Store compressed images plus thumbnails, and offer export / backup to iCloud Drive.
  • Time is hard. Recurring events, DST and all-day events across timezones. Use EventKit's expanded occurrences; never hand-roll recurrence math.
