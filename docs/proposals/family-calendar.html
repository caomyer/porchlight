One family calendar, on the wall and in every pocket

Today each device shows only the calendars set up on that device, nothing can be added in Porchlight, and the phone has no calendar view. This proposes one shared family calendar: every phone brings the calendars it already has, the wall and the other phones show them together, anyone can add an event and it lands in their real calendar, and an email or a school flyer becomes events with one share.

Revision 3 Scoped to an MVP. "Who's on it" is out of the MVP and parked while James thinks about it, so it is gone from the frames. The MVP is four things: see everyone's calendar, a Calendar tab on the phone, add and edit into your real calendar, and Free together. Everything else is listed as after.

The frames are drawn in the app's own type, colors and words, with a sample household (Alex, Sam, Mia, Leo) and the real week of Thanksgiving 2026. Nothing in them is built. Competitor facts and prices come from a web research pass on Nov 22, 2026; what could not be verified is listed at the end.

Where we are

Each device sees its own calendars, and nobody can add anything

Nothing about the calendar syncs

The wall shows the accounts added on the wall iPad. A phone shows the accounts on that phone. A family member on a second Apple ID reaches the wall only if their account is added on the iPad by hand.

Read only, everywhere

Porchlight reads the device's calendars and never writes. No event can be created, changed or removed, on the wall or the phone.

The phone has a card, not a calendar

"At home today" and "Next 7 days" on Today. No week or month, no event page (a tap leaves for Apple's Calendar), no reminders for events.

"Who" is a whole calendar

A calendar belongs to one person or to "Everyone". One event can't be marked as being for a particular person.

What is already good and stays: the wall's week, month and day views, person colors, "Busy only" and "Just me" choices, and reading through iOS, which means no Google sign-in inside Porchlight to expire.

What it is for

A household calendar fails when someone doesn't look, or when one person carries it all

The paying market is families with children under 18, with the purchase usually led by the mother. Skylight claims 1.7 million households and sells them chores, meal plans and school-email import at $79 a year on top of the screen. Couples without children are more numerous but have not shown they will pay. One design can serve both, because the core problems are shared.

Every rival signs in to Google or iCloud itself, then fights expiring sign-ins, app passwords and duplicates. Reading through iOS, as we do, avoids that whole class.

Wall products have weak phone apps

And phone apps have no wall. Nobody does both well.

The gaps we can own

A calendar that never asks you to sign in again, because iOS does the syncing. A wall and a phone that are both good. Work shown quietly, not hidden. And a view of when everyone is free, which none of them has.

The idea

Every phone brings the calendars it already has

Your work, Google and school calendars are already on your iPhone. Porchlight on each phone passes the ones you choose to the household. The wall and the other phones show one calendar. No account is ever added on the wall.

1 · Choose once

On each phone, every calendar gets one choice: Show everything, Keep it quiet (one soft block a day, for work), Busy only (times, no words) or Just me (never leaves the phone). And who it belongs to.

2 · The phone passes them on

Events from two weeks back to three months ahead go into the household's shared iCloud space, the same one chores and meals use. A second Apple ID works the same way.

3 · One calendar everywhere

The wall and every phone show the family's events together, colored by person. The same event arriving from two phones is shown once, with both faces.

Added anywhere, saved in your real calendar

The household picks one home calendar: a shared Google or iCloud calendar you already have, or a new one. An event added on a phone is written straight into it by iOS, with no Google sign-in in Porchlight, so it shows in Apple Calendar, on a watch, everywhere. Skylight and Hearth write back too.

Work, kept quiet

A work calendar becomes one soft block a day, "Alex at work until 5:30", with the meetings one tap away for the household. Your plans stay readable, and nothing reads as a secret.

Maybe After

An event can be a maybe: dashed, with a question mark. Either person confirms it or lets it go. Not in the MVP.

Adding on the wall: if the home calendar's account is on the iPad, the wall writes it directly. If not, the event shows for everyone at once through Porchlight, and a phone writes it into the home calendar the next time it runs. In the MVP the wall adds only in the first case; the hand-off through a phone comes after.

The MVP

Four things: see everyone, on the phone too, add for real, find free time

The MVP makes one promise: both of you see the same calendar, on the wall and on the phone, and anything either of you adds lands in your real calendar. It is your own stated need, and it is the base every later feature stands on.

1 · One shared calendar
  • Each phone passes on the calendars you choose.
  • Four choices per calendar: Show everything, Keep it quiet, Busy only, Just me.
  • The wall and every phone show everyone's events, colored by person, each shown once.
  • Works across two Apple IDs. The wall needs no account, and passes on any it has.
2 · Calendar tab on the phone
  • Week strip, a list that scrolls on, the month one tap away.
  • Person chips to filter.
  • An event page inside the app.
3 · Add and edit, in your real calendar
  • Choose a home calendar once.
  • Add by typing one line, on the phone.
  • Edit or delete events on the phone that owns them.
  • The wall adds too, when the home calendar's account is on the iPad.
4 · Free together
  • Coming evenings and weekend days, green where everyone is free.
  • On the phone as a second view, on the wall as a row under the week.

How we will know the MVP works: after a week on your two phones and the wall, both of you see each other's events without having opened Porchlight that day, an event added on a phone is in Apple Calendar and on the wall within a minute, and Free together matches what you would have worked out by hand.

Parked

"Who's on it" is out of the MVP

The idea: any event can name the person responsible, events for a child with nobody on them collect on the wall, and only that person is reminded. It answers a problem families with children have daily and two adults rarely have. It is removed from the frames and from the MVP, and waits for your decision. Nothing in the MVP depends on it or blocks it.

Decisions

What to approve

  1. The MVP is the four things above, and nothing else. Recommended. See everyone, the phone's Calendar tab, add and edit into your real calendar, Free together.
  2. Phones bring the calendars; no Google sign-in in Porchlight. Recommended. Free, never expires, and covers work and school calendars a sign-in can't. Revisit only if the wall turns out stale.
  3. Events are saved in a real home calendar, written by iOS on the phone. Recommended. So anything added in Porchlight is in Apple Calendar and on a watch too.
  4. "Keep it quiet" for work calendars, alongside Busy only and Just me. Recommended.
  5. Calendar becomes the phone's fifth tab, between Today and Food. Recommended. No hour grid on the phone; the wall keeps its grid.
  6. "Who's on it" stays parked until you decide.
Build order

The MVP in four steps, then what follows

1 · One shared calendar MVP
  • Phones pass on chosen calendars; the four choices enforced.
  • Wall and phone's existing views show everyone's, each event once.
  • Measures how fresh a sleeping phone keeps the wall.
  • One schema deploy from you.
2 · The phone's Calendar tab MVP
  • Week strip, list, month, person chips.
  • An event page in the app.
  • Today's card links into it.
3 · Add and edit MVP
  • Home calendar chosen once.
  • One-line add and edit on the phone, written through iOS.
  • Wall add when the account is on the iPad.
4 · Free together MVP
  • Evenings and weekend days, on phone and wall.
  • Work counts as busy, quiet or not.
Then · Share to Porchlight
  • Email text, PDF, photo, camera.
  • Read on the phone; review, then add.
After
  • Notices and clash warnings; maybe-plans.
  • Wall add through a phone.
  • Parked: "Who's on it".
  • If needed: Google on the wall.

Each step ships to TestFlight by itself, so you use the shared calendar after step 1, before the rest exists. Each follows docs/design/testing.md: merging, the four choices and free-time maths in unit tests with a pinned clock; one journey per real journey in UI tests.

Not verified

What I couldn't check, and how each gets settled

  • How fresh a sleeping phone keeps the wall. iOS decides when an app may refresh in the background. Step 1 measures it on your two phones before anything else is built on it.
  • Writing into the home calendar from the wall through a phone. Out of the MVP for this reason; writing from a phone is ordinary iOS and low risk.
  • Demand from households without children. Thin evidence: one study and a few reviews. Reddit could not be searched.
  • Matching the same event across two accounts. iOS gives events an id meant to be shared across devices; whether it holds between a Google and an iCloud copy of one invite needs a test on real accounts.
  • How well the on-device model reads real school email. Step 4 starts with a test set of real messages and a pass bar before any screen is built. The wall iPad (iPadOS 17) can't run it, so reading happens on phones.
  • Whether Gmail's own found events arrive through iOS. Expected, since they sit on the Google calendar, but untested here.
  • The second phone's model and iOS version, which decides whether it gets the smart reading or the plain one.
  • Prices and review rules for Google's security assessment and some rival plans come from third-party pages, not the vendors.
