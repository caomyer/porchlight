Porchlight · Companion UX atlas · Nov 15, 2026 · brought in line with what shipped on Nov 17

Every screen of the phone companion, journey by journey

This is the full experience for the iPhone companion and the wall changes that come with it, laid out so the work can be split. Each journey has an id, a workstream and a milestone, a storyboard you read left to right, the states it has to handle, and what "done" means.

It builds on the companion proposal (roles, iCloud sync, storage) and stays generic: nothing assumes a wall iPad, our family or a dog. Frames scroll sideways inside each journey. Sample people are James and Mia (parents with iPhones), Ava (a kid without a phone) and Yo-Yo the toy poodle, in a household called The Lake House.

The model

Five words carry the whole experience

HouseholdWhat gets shared

One family's walks, meals, chores, lists and stars. Lives in the creator's iCloud. Someone can be in more than one.

PersonSomeone in the household

Has a name and a color, and is credited for what they do. Kids and grandparents can be people without ever installing the app.

MemberA person who joined from their own Apple ID

Links an iCloud account to a person. Members can log from their phones, get reminders and be reminded.

Device roleShared screen or personal

A shared screen asks "who did it?". A personal device credits its member. A setting, never inferred from iPad versus iPhone.

OwnerThe member whose iCloud holds it

Usually whoever created it. Shown, never assumed by the data, so a future handoff needs no schema change.

Navigation

Four tabs in the pocket, the same rail on the wall

iPhone, personal

  1. Today - care due now, your chores, what anyone can take, today's agenda.
    • Companion week, Pitch in (full list), Next 7 days
  2. Food - snap a meal, this week's plates, an entry's page.
  3. Lists - groceries and other lists, this week's dinners.
  4. Glow - your stars, the family goal, cash in.
  5. You (avatar, top right on every tab) - who you are, households, invite, notifications, calendars, storage, household setup, leave.

iPad, shared screen (the wall)

  1. Today, Calendar, each companion, Food, Pitch in, Glow, Lists, Photos: as today.
  2. Settings, which gains:
    • Family & devices new: members, invite, this device's role; a join code is shown on the owner's iPhone
    • iCloud sync new: the switch, a plain status line, and Sync details
  3. New behavior on existing screens: "from Mia's phone" tags, Remind someone, cash-in celebrations.

iPad, personal

  1. The wall layout and rail, but credited to its member like a phone, with no "who did it?" step.
  2. No kiosk, night or ambient mode by default.
  3. Gets the same You menu as the phone, under Settings.

This falls out of "role is a setting" and needs no screens of its own.

Experience rules

What every screen follows

1A personal device never asks who did it. It credits its member and offers "also went" for anyone who joined in.
2One tap, then undo. Logging is a single tap with a short undo toast, never a confirmation dialog. Details are optional and come after.
3Buzz only on purpose. Notifications come only for your own routines and for someone asking you. Never for someone else's activity, never as a badge count.
4Everything works offline. Nothing shows a spinner for sync. Sync shows up only when it has been stuck long enough to matter, in plain words with one fix.
5Same words, same pieces, everywhere. A walk is called a walk on the wall, the phone, the notification, the widget and Siri. The phone reuses the wall's components (the time pill and number pad, polaroids, repeat picker) at phone size.
6Any device can set things up. People, companions, chores, rewards and lists can be edited from any member's device, with the same editors as sheets. A phone-only family must lack nothing.
7Kids stay simple. No PINs, no approval queues. Kids pitch in on a shared screen, and a kid with their own phone joins as a member like anyone else.
8Accessible by default. Dynamic Type to the largest accessibility size without truncating actions, VoiceOver labels that read the result ("Log Yo-Yo's evening walk"), 44 pt targets, and a night look that follows the system.
9The household keeps one clock. Days, routine windows and streaks follow the household's home time zone, so a parent who is traveling still sees home's Tuesday and two devices never disagree about which day a walk belongs to.
10Never confidently out of date. A notification, widget or Siri action checks the latest state before it logs. If someone already did it, it says so ("Mia walked Yo-Yo at 5:40") and offers "I went too" instead of adding a second walk.
Journeys

Six workstreams, thirty journeys

Each journey is tagged with the milestone it ships in, M1 to M5, or Later. The delivery plan at the end says what each milestone has to prove.

Divide and conquer

Sync is the risk and the screens are not, so the plan gets two real devices syncing as early as possible and widens from there. Every milestone ships to our own household and has to pass its gate before the next one starts. Each journey above carries its milestone tag.

Proven before the design depends on it

Five small experiments run during M0 and M1. Each answers a question the journeys take for granted, and each has a fallback. A sixth is already done: the app's existing way of writing data works unchanged with the sync library, proven against its stand-in for iCloud.

S1A widget or Siri log reaches iCloud without opening the app. Settled on paper: only the app itself can sync, so every logging action runs inside the app, woken in the background, and widgets read a small summary the app writes for them. A widget tap that can't wake the app is saved and sent when the app next runs. Still to be confirmed on a real phone in M4.
S2Invites and removal. Answered: a share open to anyone with its link can't also have people invited by name, and closing that link removes everyone who came through it. So Porchlight never opens a public link: a join code is one private seat, made on the owner's own iPhone (iOS 18 and later) for one person, and removing someone ends only their access (docs/design/households.md, "The join code").
S3The toolchain serves the wall. Done: the sync library's current release builds with the Xcode already on this Mac, for iPadOS 17. No Xcode upgrade is needed.
S4Cable installs and TestFlight installs share one iCloud. Apple keeps development and production data apart, so the wall and the phones must be on the same side.
S5One Apple ID, two roles. The owner's phone and a shared wall signed in to the same Apple ID must behave as one member with two devices.

Keeping the wall safe while we build

1Back up before every install. The deploy script copies the wall's data to the Mac first, and the M0 migration keeps the old database on the iPad until M1 passes.
2Sync has an off switch. M0 changes the data with iCloud off, and M1 turns it on behind a setting, so a sync problem never takes the wall down.
3The wall never waits on a phone. Every milestone leaves the wall fully working on its own.

Decisions this atlas changes or adds

QuestionRecommendationWas
Where can the household be set up?On any member's device, with the wall's editors shown as sheets. A phone-only family needs it, and one set of editors is simpler than two.Setup stays on the wall in v1.
Where can a reward be cashed in?Anywhere. Shared screens in the household celebrate it, so it still feels like a family moment.Wall only.
Phone in the dark?Follow the system night look with a warm dark palette. The wall keeps its single look and its own night mode.Not decided.
How does a phone learn who you are?Pick yourself once when joining, with people who haven't joined listed first and "I'm new here" to add yourself.Same, plus the new-person path.
Walk timer on the phone?Later. A Live Activity that times the walk and logs the duration on end. A natural phone-only feature.Not in the proposal.
What time zone is a "day"?The household's home time zone, set when it is created and changeable in Settings (rule 9).Each device's own time zone.
A reminder that is out of date?Check first, then say what happened (rule 10). iOS can't always withdraw a reminder in time, so the action itself has to be safe.Withdrawn on next sync only.
In what order does it ship?Six milestones with gates, core loop on two real phones first.Three phases, most journeys in Phase 1.
How does someone join with a code?One private seat per showing, made on the owner's own iPhone (iOS 18 and later) and good for one person. The iPadOS 17 wall and members' phones say where to find it. Never a public link.A QR code of the household's link on the wall.
Siri and Shortcuts?With widgets, in M4, since the App Intents behind the widget and notification actions give Siri for free.Not in the proposal.
