Porchlight · Time field · Options to choose from

Four ways to change a time

Every option starts with the field's time already filled in, so you only change the part that's wrong. None has a colon key. Each one is live below. The "now" in this mock is 7:40 PM on a dinner log, so the field starts at 7:40 PM, and times later than that can't be saved.

What the common patterns do
Prefilled, edit in place

Apple's compact picker, Android's input mode and the web's time input all open on the current value and let you replace the hour or the minutes on their own.

Typing plus a picker

Design systems suggest offering typed numbers alongside a visual picker. Typing is fastest for an exact time and works with assistive tools.

Wheels are Apple's default

iOS uses scrolling wheels for times. They are familiar but can overshoot, and they need precise flicks.

The dial is Android's

Material's clock face: tap an hour, then the minutes. SAP's user study rated a dial highly, with a typing popover offered alongside it.

Try each one
iPad clock
A

Hour and minute boxes with a number padRecommended

Like Android's input mode, iOS's compact time and the web's time input

Meal
  • Any exact time in two to four taps. To fix 7:40 to 7:15, tap the minutes, then 1 5.
  • The hour is selected when it opens, so typing replaces it and then moves on to the minutes. Each digit counts the moment it's typed: 2 to 1 is one tap.
  • A hardware keyboard works too: type digits, use the arrows to switch boxes or nudge.
  • The tallest card of the four, because of the number pad.
B

Up and down buttons

Like alarm and oven clocks, and Apple Watch's crown

Meal
  • The smallest card. Best for "a little earlier": two taps take 7:40 to 7:30.
  • Nothing to read or type. Big targets for a wall-mounted iPad.
  • Far times take many taps: 7:40 PM to 12:15 PM is seven hour taps and five minute taps.
  • Minutes move in fives, so 7:43 can't be set.
C

Scrolling wheels

Apple's wheel picker, on iPhone and iPad

Meal
  • The one most iPad owners already know. One flick covers a far change.
  • Every minute is reachable.
  • Easy to overshoot, and it needs a careful flick at arm's length on a wall.
  • It's what the app moved away from in the last change.
D

Clock face

Android's Material dial

Meal
  • Any time in two touches: tap the hour, and it switches to minutes for the second.
  • Pleasant to glance at, and it fits the clock on the photo frame.
  • Android in look and feel; unfamiliar on an iPad.
  • Odd minutes need a careful drag. 24-hour needs a second ring of hours.

Click a card first, then a hardware keyboard works on it: digits, Backspace, arrows, A and P. Done shows what would be kept.

Decisions
QuestionRecommendationWhy
Which pickerA, with the suggestions kept on topExact times stay fast, the field starts filled in, and it is the pattern on Android, the web and iOS's compact picker.
What it opens onThe field's time, with the hour selectedYou change only what's wrong. Tapping the minutes selects them instead.
The colon keyRemoved; the bottom row is blank, 0, deleteThe boxes already split hours from minutes, like a phone keypad.
When a digit countsAt once, every time2 then 1 makes the hour 1 right away, and Done or tapping the minutes keeps it. Nothing typed is ever thrown away.
Hours that can have two digits"1" stays open for one more digit1 then 0, 1 or 2 makes 10, 11 or 12. Any other digit goes on to the minutes, so 1 5 0 is 1:50 and 7 1 5 is 7:15. For 1:15, type 1, tap the minutes, then 1 5 (1 1 5 is 11:05, the same as the web's time input). On a 24-hour clock 0, 1 and 2 stay open the same way.
After the hour is typedMove to the minutes on their ownOnce the hour can't take another digit, the minutes are selected. Minutes work the same way: 4 sets :04, then 5 makes it :45.
AM or PM when typing an hourKeep what is shownThe field starts on a sensible time, so the half of the day is usually right already. The AM | PM switch fixes the rest, and a time later than now still gets its reason.
Up/down nudges on A's boxesNot nowSuggestions and typing already cover small changes. Easy to add later if "5 minutes earlier" turns out to be common.
