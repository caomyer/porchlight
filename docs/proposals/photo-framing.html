What you aim at should be what the wall shows

When you snap a meal, the camera shows a tall, narrow slice of the scene. The photo is saved as a wide 4:3 frame, and the wall shows a square from its middle. Those are three different crops, so the polaroid never matches what you framed. This page shows why, and proposes one square frame from the shutter to the wall.

The camera fills its tall panel, so it crops away the sides of its wide frame. Then the shutter saves the whole frame, and the wall cuts a square from it. The square is nearly twice as wide as what you saw, so dishes you framed out come back, and what you centered in the tall slice ends up small in the middle.

The fix in one line

One square frame, from the shutter to the wall

Polaroids, thumbnails and the week grid are all square, and they should stay square so the wall stays tidy. So the camera should show exactly that square. What you see in the viewfinder is the polaroid you get.

What does the camera show?Exactly that squareWhat you aim at is what you keep. The brackets go away; the square's edges are the frame.

How it would be built
  • The live preview sits in a square window, so it shows the middle square of the camera frame, top to bottom. On save, the app records that same square, so the viewfinder and the polaroid match exactly.
  • Each photo keeps its full file and gets a crop (a square's position and size, as fractions of the photo). A migration gives existing photos the center square they show today.
  • The thumbnail and a square display copy are made from the crop when the photo is saved or reframed, so grids load as fast as today.
  • Reframing is a drag in a square window, saved with the rest of the edit.
  • Tests: the saved crop equals the viewfinder's square, picked photos start centered, reframing changes the thumbnail, and existing photos keep their look. I'll also check the capture sheet on the iPad's screen with photos added and the keyboard up.
