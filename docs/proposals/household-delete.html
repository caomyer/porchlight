Deleting a household for good

An owner can end a household: everything it holds leaves their iCloud and every device that shared it, and nobody can bring it back. Today the phone only says "It's yours, so it stays with you", and the one way to delete is buried in iOS Settings, where it deletes every household the owner has at once.

This is the release checklist's item "Deleting a household from iCloud for good" (docs/design/release-checklist.md). It adds one owner-only action to the phone's household page, and says what everyone else sees. The sample is the atlas's: James owns The Lake House, Mia has an iPhone, Ava uses the hallway iPad, and there are two years of walks and meals with 412 MB of photos.

The rules

Rare, deliberate, and honest about what goes

1Only the owner, only from their own device. The household lives in the owner's iCloud, and only they can delete it. Members can leave; shared screens, which anyone in the hallway can touch, never offer it.
2Say exactly what goes, before anything goes. Who loses it, what is deleted, how much, and that nobody can undo it, in plain words, with the real names and numbers.
3One deliberate step, not a quick tap. Typing the household's name is the confirmation: hard to do by accident, easy to do on purpose, and it reads back what is being deleted.
4Online or not at all. Deleting is never queued for later. Without iCloud the button explains why it can't, so nothing is deleted at a surprising moment.
5Everyone else finds out calmly. Members and shared screens see one screen that says what happened, then the welcome. Nothing of the household stays behind on any device.

States

Every way it can go

Before deleting

  • Offline, or iCloud can't be reached: the button is off and the sheet says "Deleting needs iCloud. Connect to the internet and try again."
  • iCloud off for Porchlight, or signed out: the household is only on this iPhone, so the sheet says so, and deleting takes it off this iPhone (nobody else has it).
  • The owner's iCloud is full: deleting still works; it frees the room.
  • Someone else is logging right now: deleting wins; their change is refused by iCloud and their phone shows the deleted screen.
  • Not the owner: no button; "Only James can delete The Lake House" and Leave.
  • A shared screen, even the owner's: no button; it says where to do it.

While deleting

  • Deleting: "Deleting The Lake House…", the sheet can't be swiped away, and the rest of the app waits behind it.
  • The connection drops: "Couldn't delete it. Nothing was deleted. Check that this iPhone is online, then try again." iCloud deletes the household's zone in one step, so it is never half deleted.
  • The app is closed mid-way: on the next launch it asks iCloud whether the household still exists and shows either the household or the deleted screen.

Afterwards

  • The owner's phone: "The Lake House is deleted", then the welcome, or the next household when it belongs to more than one (A5).
  • Members' phones: the deleted screen once, at their next sync, then the welcome or their next household; reminders for it are withdrawn.
  • Shared screens: the same screen at wall size, then the iPad's setup.
  • The owner's other devices on the same Apple ID: the same as members.
  • Offline devices: keep showing it until they reach iCloud, then the deleted screen; anything logged meanwhile is dropped with it.
  • Widgets: "Join or start a household in Porchlight."
For James

Decisions to approve

1 · The confirmationType the household's name recommended

Deliberate and readable: the field shows what is being deleted. Alternatives: a second alert ("Delete for everyone?"), which people tap through, or press and hold, which is hard with VoiceOver and Switch Control.

2 · WhereThe owner's phone only recommended

The wall is a shared screen anyone can touch, and iPadOS 17 there can't ask "are you the owner" any better than a hallway visitor can answer it. A personal iPad on the owner's Apple ID gets the same row as the phone.

3 · A way backNone, said plainly recommended

A grace period would mean keeping deleted data in someone's iCloud, which is exactly what the owner asked to stop. A "save a copy first" export is a separate feature, if ever.

4 · What members are told"James deleted The Lake House, or removed this iPhone from it" recommended

To a member, iCloud reports a deleted household the same way as being removed from it. The words name both rather than guess; a marker written before deleting could tell them apart, but only for devices that sync in between.

Build notes

How it would work

  • The household is one record zone in the owner's private iCloud database, with the share at its root. Deleting the zone deletes every record, every photo asset and the share in one operation, and iCloud then reports the share as gone to every participant.
  • On the owner's device: check the account and reachability first (the button's state), delete the zone through the sync engine, then remove the household's local data and files the way leaving does today (`AppModel.removeHouseholdHere`, `AppFiles.removeHouseholdFiles`), and withdraw its reminders and widget summary.
  • On every other device: the existing "removed" path (journey A6, `RemovedHousehold`) already takes the household off and shows one screen; this only changes its words.
  • The phone's household page gains one row for the owner and one line for members (`PocketHousehold`), the leave sheet's owner text points to it, and the wall's Family & devices gains one line for the owner (`FamilySection`).
  • Tests: the owner deletes (sheet, typed name, offline and not-owner states) against the fake sharing service, a member sees the deleted screen, and the wall's line; unit tests that deleting removes every table's rows and files for that household only, and never another household on the same device.
  • The release checklist's privacy policy then says: delete a household from You › the household › Delete; to delete everything Porchlight keeps in your iCloud at once, iOS Settings › your name › iCloud › Manage Account Storage › Porchlight › Delete Data.
