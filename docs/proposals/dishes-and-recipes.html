Dishes and recipes

A meal can say what was on the table, one dish at a time, and every dish remembers itself: where we had it, how each of us liked it, and, when we cook it, the recipe. Logging stays exactly as fast as today, because a meal with no dishes is still a whole meal.

The sample is the atlas's household, The Lake House: James and Mia have iPhones, Ava (9) uses the kitchen iPad. They dine out at Pho Saigon House, order from Tony's Corner Pizza and Sakura Sushi Bar, and cook pesto pasta, chicken curry and miso salmon at home. Every frame uses the app's real words, type, colors and layout; nothing here is built.

Shape A · proposedA meal holds dish lines recommended

One meal is one table: one set of photos, one place, one time, the same people. Dishes are lines on it, each pointing at a dish that remembers itself. Logging three dishes is three words, not three meals, and the week's grid keeps one polaroid per meal.

Shape B · consideredEvery meal is one dish not proposed

Tempting, because a meal row already has a name, a face and photos. But three dishes would be three meals, each asking where, when and who; the table's one photo would belong to one of them; and fixing "who ate" would mean fixing three rows.

  • A dish belongs to one kitchen. "Pho tai" at Pho Saigon House and "Pho tai" made at home are two dishes, so each has its own history and faces; each page says "Also had at …".
  • A recipe is not a second kind of food. It is what a home dish knows about how to make it, so cooking a dish links to its recipe by itself, and "cooked 6 times" is simply that dish's home meals.

Requirements

What has to be true, each one testable

Logging stays fast

  1. L1Snap and Save, with nothing typed, saves a complete meal in the same taps as today, on the phone and the wall.
  2. L2With one dish typed, the form looks like today's plus one "+ Another dish" link; nothing else is asked.
  3. L3Faces are optional on every dish; a meal saves with none.
  4. L4Suggestions appear within one keystroke, from this device, with no network.
  5. L5Choosing a restaurant offers what we had there last time as one-tap chips.
  6. L6A meal holds up to 12 dishes; each name is up to 60 characters.

The app remembers

  1. M1At a restaurant, suggestions list that restaurant's dishes first, then the same words anywhere else.
  2. M2At home, suggestions list home dishes first, recipes marked, most cooked first.
  3. M3Typing a name that matches a dish in the same kitchen, ignoring case, accents and spaces, uses that dish instead of making a new one.
  4. M4Every dish has a page: times had, first and last, each person's usual face, photos, every meal, its kitchen, and its recipe for a home dish.
  5. M5Every restaurant has a page: visits, last time's dishes, and everything we had there ranked by our faces.
  6. M6Search finds dishes, restaurants, recipes and ingredients from one field.
  7. M7A dish can be starred as a household favourite, and favourites can be listed on their own.

Faces

  1. F1One tap gives a dish the table's face, for everyone who ate.
  2. F2"Rate each" lets each person who ate give their own face, on any device, later.
  3. F3A person's own face overrides the table's for them; the dish page shows each person's.
  4. F4The meal's face, seen by older builds and in the diary, is the best face among its dishes.

Recipes (second step)

  1. R1A recipe is added from the recipe bank or from a home dish's page, with a name, and optionally serves, ingredients, steps, a link, card photos and notes.
  2. R2A recipe named like an existing home dish joins that dish and its history.
  3. R3"We made this" opens Snap with Home-cooked and the dish already filled in.
  4. R4Ingredients can be ticked and added to the grocery list, one item per line, in its aisle.
  5. R5A planned dinner can name a home dish; its "What does it need?" starts from the recipe.
  6. R6The wall shows a recipe readable from across the kitchen, ingredients beside steps.

Fixing things

  1. E1A dish line can be added to, renamed on, or taken off a saved meal from Edit.
  2. E2Renaming a dish changes it on every meal; renaming to a name already in that kitchen offers to merge.
  3. E3Removing a dish takes it off its meals after saying how many; the meals and photos stay.
  4. E4Removing a recipe keeps its dish, the dish's meals and any planned dinner.
  5. E5Every remove offers Undo, as removing a grocery item does.

Sharing and older builds

  1. S1Dishes, faces and recipes are household data: they sync to every member and shared screen, and never cross into another household.
  2. S2Everything works offline and syncs later; two devices that add the same new dish end with one.
  3. S3On upgrade, every named meal gets one dish line, and every device computes the same rows.
  4. S4An older build shows every meal as today, with the dishes joined in its title and the best face.

What changes

For existing data, and for existing screens

Existing data
  • Every meal with a typed title gets one dish line, its face moved onto it. Meals titled with their slot name get none.
  • Meals with the same title in the same kitchen share one dish, so history is there on day one: "Pho, 14 times".
  • Migrated dishes and lines take ids derived from the household, the kitchen and the name (and the meal), so every device that upgrades writes the same rows, and they merge.
  • Two meals in one slot stay two meals. Nothing is combined behind anyone's back.
  • Planned dinners stay as they are; in step 2 a dinner whose words match a home dish links to it.
  • No meal, photo, title or face is deleted or rewritten except to add the dish lines.
Existing screens
  • Snap details (phone PocketSnap, wall CaptureSheet): Where moves above "What was it?", which grows dish lines; "Would eat again?" shows only for a meal with no dishes.
  • A meal's page (PocketMealDetail, MealDetailSheet): "What we had" with each dish's face, and "Rate each". "Another dinner dish" becomes "Another meal at dinner".
  • Edit meal: the same dish lines.
  • Food tab (phone): Diary and Dishes, then Recipes in step 2. The wall's Week and Month gain Dishes, then Recipes.
  • Polaroids and the diary: the first dish's name, with "+2" for more.
  • Lists › Dinners and the wall's dinner editor (step 2): suggestions from what we cook, and a recipe's ingredients for "What does it need?".

For James

Decisions to approve

1 · The shapeA meal holds dish lines recommended

One table is one meal, with its photos, place and people said once; dishes are lines on it. The alternative, each meal being one dish, would make three dishes cost three meals and split the table's photo from two of them.

2 · FacesThe table's face per dish, and each person's when they differ recommended

One tap keeps logging fast and matches today's verdict; "Rate each" captures what a family actually argues about (Ava and spice). Per person only would ask the logger to rate for three people every time; one face only would never learn that Ava doesn't like bun cha.

3 · What a dish isA name in one kitchen recommended

Pho at two places is two dishes with their own faces, linked by name. One dish across all kitchens would mix "great here, bad there", which is exactly what "what do we get here?" must tell apart.

4 · Where comes firstMove Where above "What was it?" recommended

The memory needs the kitchen before the first keystroke. It costs nothing at home (Home-cooked is already chosen) and one tap out, which logging already asks for. Keeping today's order would offer last time's dishes only after you had typed them.

5 · Existing mealsOne dish line per named meal; no combining recommended

History is there on day one and nothing changes behind anyone's back. Combining today's same-slot meals (pizza and garlic bread) into one would move photos between meals and surprise older builds.

6 · A recipeBelongs to a home dish; ingredients and steps are plain lines recommended

The simplest thing a family keeps up: paste it, type it, or snap the card. Lines are enough for ticking groceries. Quantities, units and scaling would make every recipe a form to fill, and nobody fills forms for dinner.

7 · Two steps, two schema deploysShip dishes first, then recipes recommended

Dishes are useful alone and teach us how the bank should feel; recipe tables wait until they are built ("structure is earned"). It means two CloudKit deploys, each a few minutes of James's time, and deploys only ever add.

8 · Older buildsDish lines are the truth; update every device, wall first recommended

Older builds keep working from the title and the best face, and never break. An edit made on one is overwritten by the dishes on the next save from a newer build. Detecting and merging such edits would be fragile for a household of three devices.

Deliberately out of scope

Not part of this

Nutrition

No calories, macros or diets. This is a family's memory, not a tracker.

Structured ingredients

No quantities, units, conversions or serving scaling. Lines only.

A pantry

No stock of what is in the cupboard; the grocery list stays the one list.

Automatic plans

No generated week of dinners. The bank suggests; people plan.

Menus and reviews

No restaurant menus or public ratings from the internet; only what we ate and what we thought.

Sharing outside

No public recipes, no social sharing, no cross-household bank.

Build notes

How it would work

  • Tables (step 1): dish (household, name, emoji, tint, kitchen: home, place or elsewhere, place id, favourite, merged into, created at); mealDish (meal, dish id, order, the table's face); mealDishRating (dish line, person, face, id derived from the two). Each follows the schema rules: text ids, one cascading parent, no unique indexes, plain ids for cross links.
  • Tables (step 2): recipe (id derived from its dish: one per dish; serves, ingredients, steps, link, notes, updated at), recipePhoto with its image and thumbnail tables (the meal photo pipeline), and mealPlan.dishId.
  • Writes: FoodStore.addMeal and updateMeal take dish lines and write the title and face from them in the same transaction; the upgrade is a migration that writes derived-id rows, and adopting an older build's meal uses the same function.
  • Merging moves lines and faces to the surviving dish in one transaction and leaves the other row marked; readers follow the mark. The same rule runs when sync delivers a duplicate.
  • Suggestions are one query over the household's dishes, ordered by kitchen match, then use count, then last eaten, matched by the normalized name already used for illustrations (DishIllustration.normalized).
  • Older builds: mirrors already read enums and JSON as strings; what an older build does with record types it has no table for must be proven with a sync test against the fake iCloud before step 1 ships. If it can't ignore them safely, step 1 waits for every device to update.
  • CloudKit: each step's record types are seeded into Development with scripts/cloud-schema.sh seed, exported to Config/cloudkit-schema.ckdb for review, and deployed to Production by James in the CloudKit Console before the build reaches anyone.
  • Tests: unit tests for normalization, kitchen moves, merging (including late lines), the upgrade on a copy of the wall's data, faces summaries and grocery lines; UI tests for logging with zero, one and several dishes on both shells, "Rate each" on the wall, rename and merge, and removing a recipe.
